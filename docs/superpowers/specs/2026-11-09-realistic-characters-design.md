# Realistic characters and animation — design

Date: 2026-11-09 · Status: design approved by the owner in chat; this written spec awaits the owner's review

Sub-project 3 of the visual overhaul (see `2026-11-09-over-the-shoulder-camera-design.md`, "Context").

## Decisions this builds on

- **Look:** photoreal, in the spirit of God of War, entirely our own. **Assets:** CC0 plus our own work
  only; every file under `/assets` has a ledger row.
- **Camera:** the close over-the-shoulder camera, so characters are seen mostly from behind at about 4 m.
- **Route:** bodies from MPFB (MakeHuman) in Blender on one skeleton, clips from the free Quaternius
  Universal Animation Library, retargeted with Godot's humanoid bone map (master plan, "Your heroes").
- **The pipeline test (9 Nov)** proved that route with no manual step. Blender 5.2.3 and MPFB 2.0.18, run
  with no window, build a rigged, armoured human in about 2 s; Godot 4.7.2 imports and retargets it and
  both clip packs with no editor open. Its throwaway files are in `tools/out/character_test/`.
- **The owner's answers (9 Nov):**
  - Faces: heroes wear a helm or hood on expeditions. Faces show at camp and get a modest pass.
  - Scope: both heroes and the three arena enemies (thrall, archer, knight).
  - Clips: the free clips only for now. The Crypt Archer carries a hand crossbow so the free one-handed
    aiming clips fit. Revisit once it has been seen moving.

## Goal

Replace the placeholder figures of both heroes and the three arena enemies with realistic,
skeleton-animated characters. They are built by a scripted, repeatable pipeline, animated only from
state the game already has, and add at most 1.5 ms a frame in a full fight.

## Non-goals

- No change to simulation, gameplay data, the network format or saves. No new replicated state.
- The Bellkeeper, the Drowned Abbess, her anchors, the training dummy and the barrels keep their
  placeholder models. The two bosses belong to sub-project 5.
- No cloth simulation, facial animation, foot placement on slopes or paired finisher animations.
- No strafing or backpedal clips: both are approximated (see "Locomotion").
- No clip for a hero reviving their partner or for a successful parry: no state marks either today.
- No likeness tuning and no camp scenes. The definitions make both possible later.
- No level-of-detail work beyond Godot's automatic mesh LODs.

### Definitions

- `art/characters/<id>.json`, one per character: body settings (MPFB's gender, age, muscle, weight,
  proportions and height values), skin, hair, eyes, eyebrows and eyelashes by asset name, outfit pieces,
  headgear, props and which props are the signature weapon.
- An outfit piece names its helper surface, the bones whose vertices it keeps, its cut planes (points
  along named bones or heights), how far it is pushed out, its thickness, its material slot and the body
  region it hides. Body faces under an opaque piece are deleted at build time.
- `art/characters/clips_extra.json` describes the extra clips (see "Clips").
- `art/` holds a `.gdignore`, so Godot never scans it.

### Import settings

`AGENTS.md` says import settings change only through the editor. The retarget settings below cannot be
set that way by a script, so this design asks for one exception, recorded in `AGENTS.md`: **the
`.import` files of character and clip GLBs are written by `write_imports.gd` and never by hand.**

- **Characters:** scene importer; on the skeleton, `retarget/bone_map` (`mpfb_bone_map.tres`) and
  `retarget/rest_fixer/fix_silhouette/enable = true` (MPFB bodies rest in an A-pose, the clips assume a
  T-pose); outfit material slots point at the shared materials through `use_external`; animation import
  off; automatic LODs and shadow meshes on; `import_script/path` set to the post-import script.
- **Clip files:** animation-library importer; `retarget/bone_map` (`quaternius_bone_map.tres`) and
  `retarget/remove_tracks/unmapped_bones = true`.
- **Bone maps** map by name. Both rigs use Unreal-style names (`pelvis`, `spine_01`, `upperarm_l` and so
  on), so one table serves both. They live in `game/characters/import/`.
- **Textures.** Headless import leaves loose 3D textures uncompressed and without mipmaps (the textures
  session's finding). Character textures therefore stay inside the GLB and are embedded as Basis
  Universal (`gltf/embedded_image_handling = 2`). A test asserts they are compressed and have mipmaps.
  If it fails, the first pull request stops and reports; nobody hand-edits an import.
- **Post-import script** (`character_post_import.gd`, an `EditorScenePostImport`): sets every mesh to
  `GI_MODE_DISABLED`; makes skin opaque with subsurface scattering, hair and brow cards alpha-scissored
  with alpha-to-coverage, eyes glossy; records the built height as scene metadata; for enemies, adds the
  physical bones (see "Ragdolls"); and fails the import with a clear message if the scene breaks a rule
  (more than three skinned meshes, a missing `GeneralSkeleton`, a missing prop).

### Findings from the test this design depends on

- Godot removes a trailing `_Loop` from clip names on import (`Idle_Loop` becomes `Idle`).
- A character whose meshes use `GI_MODE_DYNAMIC` cost about 2.4 ms each in the hall. With
  `GI_MODE_DISABLED` nine characters cost about 1 ms in total and still receive bounced light.
- Nine characters of 18 skinned meshes each overflowed Direct3D 12's resource descriptor heap. Merged
  into one skinned mesh each, the error is gone.
- Bone attachments added before a paused clip's first pose left the skeleton in its rest pose. Props
  are part of the imported scene, and animation runs every frame, so this does not arise in play.
- A script run with `-s` is compiled before the autoloads exist. The review scene runs as a scene.

### Data

Both tables live in `data/tables/` and load through `GameData`. Like every table there, they are part of
the content hash, so both PCs update together.

- `character_roster.csv`: `for_kind` (`class` or `enemy`), `for_id`, `character`, `clip_set`. A class or
  enemy without a row keeps its placeholder.
- `character_clips.csv`: `clip_set`, `role`, `clip` (`ual1/<name>`, `ual2/<name>` or `extra/<name>`),
  `start_s`, `contact_s`, `end_s`, `layer` (`full`, `upper` or `upper_when_moving`), `loop`, `speed_mps`
  (for walk cycles), `notes`.
- Roles are stable keys. Base roles: `idle`, `walk`, `jog`, `sprint`, `block`, `downed_idle`,
  `downed_crawl`, `dead`, `stunned`, `staggered`, `prone`, `airborne`, `spawn`. One-shot roles: `dodge`,
  `get_up`, `hit`, `knockback`, `death`, `finisher` and `ability:<ability_id>`. An ability with no row
  falls back to `delivery:<delivery>`, then to `action_default`.

### Timing: the game's clock decides

Ability timing stays in data, in ticks (the master plan's rule). A clip is stretched to fit it.

- For an action with windup `W`, active `A` and recovery `R` ticks, and elapsed time `e` seconds since
  `action_tick` (using `NetworkTime.tick_factor`), the clip time is:
  - while `e <= W/60`: `start_s + (contact_s - start_s) * e / (W/60)`
  - after that: `contact_s + (end_s - contact_s) * min(1, (e - W/60) / ((A + R)/60))`
- So the contact frame lands on the tick the active window opens, on both PCs, and a rollback cannot
  leave the pose out of step. An action with no windup starts at its contact frame.
- The clip time is computed from state every frame, not left to run, so a cancelled or restarted action
  needs no special handling.
- A test flags any row whose windup would play slower than 0.4× or faster than 3×, so a better section
  of the clip is chosen instead.

### Hero poses

`HeroAnimDriver` picks the first rule that applies:

| Order | When | Shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `life` is Fallen | `dead`, held on its last frame |
| 2 | `motor.downed` or `life` is Downed | `downed_crawl` while moving, otherwise `downed_idle`; `get_up` once when life returns to Alive |
| 3 | A finisher is playing (`play_finisher`) | `finisher`, its contact at `finisher_impact_ticks` of `finisher_lock_ticks` |
| 4 | Dodging, or within 10 ticks after `dodge_ticks` end | `dodge`, full body: its contact frame marks the end of the tumble and lands when the dodge ends; the rest blends into movement |
| 5 | `action_slot >= 0` | `ability:<id>` from `motor.current_action_def()`; upper body over moving legs when the row says `upper_when_moving` and speed is above 0.5 m/s, otherwise full body |
| 6 | `motor.blocking` | `block`: upper body while moving, full body when still. The first `parry_window_ticks` play its quick raise |
| 7 | Otherwise | Locomotion |

- `current_action_def()` is used rather than `ability_at(action_slot)`, which returns the recall during
  a throw's recovery.
- Being hit adds the `hit` role as a short upper-body overlay, started from `HeroVisual.flash()`.
- The signature weapon is hidden while `motor.weapon_out` is true.

### Locomotion

- `idle`, `walk`, `jog` and `sprint` blend by ground speed: `idle` sits at 0 and each cycle at its
  `speed_mps`. The leading cycle plays at actual speed over its `speed_mps`, within 0.6× to 1.5×, so feet
  slide as little as the clips allow.
- The free clips only move forward. When a hero moves in another direction than they face (lock-on
  strafing), the hips turn toward the movement and the spine turns back, up to 100° either way. Beyond
  that the cycle plays in reverse with the hips turned the remaining angle. This is the approximation the
  owner accepted.

### Enemy poses

`EnemyAnimDriver` maps the replicated `GameTypes.Anim` value:

| `anim` | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| `IDLE`, `MOVE` | `idle`; `walk` at a rate from the distance moved |
| `TELEGRAPH`, `ATTACK`, `RECOVER` | The attack role, stretched as for heroes with `W = telegraph_ticks`, `A = active_ticks`, `R = recovery_ticks` from the enemy definition (or the windup length a telegraph event carried). Elapsed time runs from when `TELEGRAPH` was first seen; when `ATTACK` arrives it is set to `W`, so the contact frame is right even if the guest saw the telegraph late |
| `STUNNED`, `STAGGERED`, `PRONE`, `LAUNCHED`, `SPAWNING` | `stunned`, `staggered`, `prone`, `airborne`, `spawn` |
| `KNOCKBACK` | `knockback` |
| `FROZEN` | The current pose held (`delta` 0) under the existing frozen overlay |
| `DEAD` | `death`, unless the death event starts a ragdoll |

### Ragdolls

- Only the three enemies ragdoll. The post-import script gives each enemy scene eleven physical bones
  (hips, torso, head, upper and lower arms, upper and lower legs) with capsule shapes from bone lengths,
  on physics layer 5 (bit 16) with mask `1 | 16`, as the physics claim requires. They are inactive until
  `CharacterRig.start_ragdoll(fling)`.
- The pool keeps its job: the cap, the lifetime and the sink. It gains one entry point for a rig (see
  the table below). Box ragdolls for placeholder enemies are unchanged.

## Budgets

- **Frame time:** in the review scene's `cost` sheet (the Drowned Hall on High, 3440×1440, the owner's
  PC) two heroes and ten enemies, all animating, add at most 1.5 ms to the frame time of the empty hall.
  Until all five characters exist, the sheet fills the twelve places with the ones that do.
- **Meshes:** at most three skinned meshes per character, each with `GI_MODE_DISABLED`.
- **Triangles:** as in "The cast", read from the manifests.

## Testing

**New GUT unit tests** (`tests/unit/`):

- `test_character_kit.gd`:
  - every roster row's scene loads and has a `GeneralSkeleton` with the 53 mapped bones
  - at most three skinned meshes, all `GI_MODE_DISABLED`; triangle counts within budget
  - textures are compressed and have mipmaps
  - each manifest's definition hash matches its definition file (a changed definition needs a rebuild)
  - `hero_rig()` and `enemy_rig()` return `null` when disabled and for ids without a row
- `test_character_clips.gd`:
  - every ability of both classes, and every base and one-shot role, resolves to a row for each clip set
    that needs it
  - every `clip` exists in its library; `0 <= start_s <= contact_s <= end_s <=` the clip's length
  - no windup plays outside 0.4× to 3× for the ability timings in data
- `test_hero_anim_driver.gd`:
  - the contact frame is returned exactly at `action_tick + windup_ticks`, for a light, a heavy, a
    zero-windup recall and an ability
  - the rule order: fallen, downed, finisher, dodge, action, block, locomotion
  - a throw's recovery shows the throw, not the recall
  - the action layer is upper body only when moving and the row allows it
- `test_enemy_anim_driver.gd`:
  - every `GameTypes.Anim` value maps to a role that has a row
  - `ATTACK` arriving late still puts the contact frame at the start of the active window
  - a new generation resets the timing
- `test_character_rig.gd`:
  - `parts` covers every mesh; signature props hide and show; headgear swaps with the full hair
  - `set_outline()` sets and clears; `set_accent()` changes only that rig
  - `start_ragdoll()` activates bodies on layer 16 only
  - `animator.apply()` with `delta` 0 leaves every bone pose unchanged

## Risks

- **Clip fit.** The free clips were not made for these moves. Some stretched swings may look snappy or
  odd, and strafing is approximate. Each role is reviewed on the `moves` sheet, the table makes fixes
  cheap, and the full Quaternius packs (about $25, also CC0) remain an option for the owner.
- **Faces** look dated up close. The helm and hood are the chosen mitigation; a face pass waits for camp.
- **Low-polygon proxies and the hood and helm generators** were not part of the test. If a proxy does not
  fit the cut outfit cleanly, the enemy uses the full body with a decimation step instead.
- **Basis Universal embedding** was not part of the test. The texture test in the first pull request
  decides it, with the stop rule above.
- **Cross-session ownership.** The files in the changes table belong to other sessions or are shared,
  and most hold uncommitted work. The changes there are requests until their owners agree.
